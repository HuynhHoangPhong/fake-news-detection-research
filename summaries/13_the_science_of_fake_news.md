This article discusses the growing concern over fake news in modern society and its potential impact on democracy. The authors argue that while fake news may not always change people's beliefs, it can reinforce existing false beliefs, increase their salience, or shape the news agenda in harmful ways.

The article highlights several key points:

1. Fake news is becoming a significant issue, with evidence suggesting it played a role in the 2016 US election and other high-profile events.

2. The media landscape has changed dramatically since the mid-20th century, with the rise of social media platforms that prioritize user engagement over providing objective information.

3. There is a need for interdisciplinary research spanning psychology, computer science, political science, economics, law, and communication to better understand and address the problem of fake news.

4. The article draws parallels between concerns about fake news today and earlier worries about propaganda during World War II, highlighting that even if fake news doesn't change people's beliefs, it can still have harmful effects on society.

5. The authors suggest that a multidisciplinary research program similar to the post-World War II effort aimed at countering propaganda effects is needed to address the underlying pathologies revealed by fake news.

6. They call for redesigning our news ecosystem in the 21st century, requiring new science and practices to provide objective, credible information.

7. The article discusses potential interventions, including platform-based policies that could help mitigate the spread of fake news, as well as government regulation or tort lawsuits.

Overall, the authors argue that addressing the problem of fake news requires a comprehensive approach involving multiple disciplines and stakeholders, with the goal of promoting more accurate and trustworthy information in society.
