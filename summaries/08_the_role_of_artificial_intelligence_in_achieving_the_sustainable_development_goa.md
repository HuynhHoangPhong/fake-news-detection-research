This paper explores the relationship between artificial intelligence (AI) and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The authors conducted a consensus-based expert elicitation process to assess whether AI acts as an enabler or inhibitor for each of the 17 SDGs.

Key findings:
- For 128 out of 169 targets, there is published evidence of AI acting as an enabler.
- For 41 targets, there is evidence of AI acting as an inhibitor.
- The percentage of positive and negative impacts varies across different SDG categories (Society, Economy, Environment).

The authors categorized the references based on their methodological approach:
- Type A: Sophisticated tools and data with generalizability
- Type B: Data-based but limited generalizability
- Type C: Anecdotal qualitative studies
- Type D: Purely theoretical or speculative

Limitations include potential biases due to varying amounts of references for different targets, and the fact that some literature on AI's impact on certain SDGs may have been overlooked.

The study provides a comprehensive overview of how AI is being used across various SDG areas, highlighting both enabling and inhibiting effects. However, it acknowledges that the SDGs are political compromises and may not fully capture complex dynamics and cross-interactions among targets.

This research offers valuable insights into the role of AI in sustainable development efforts and underscores the need for continued monitoring and adaptation of AI applications to better align with global goals.
