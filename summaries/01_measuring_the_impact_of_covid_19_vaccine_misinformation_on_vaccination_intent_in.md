The study you've described examines the impact of COVID-19 misinformation on vaccination intent in both the UK and the USA. Here are some key points from the methodology section:

### Ethical Approval
Ethical approval was obtained by the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine ethics committee on June 15, 2020.

### Sample Size and Recruitment
- **Total Respondents:** 8,001
- **Recruitment Method:** Online panel via ORB (Gallup) International.
- **Survey Period:** September 7-14, 2020

### Quotas
Respondent quotas were set according to national demographic distributions for gender, age, and sub-national region:
- **USA:** Four census regions
- **UK:** First level of nomenclature of territorial units

### Treatment and Control Groups
- **Treatment Group (Misinformation):** 3,000 UK and 3,001 US respondents exposed to COVID-19 vaccine-related online misinformation.
- **Control Group (Factual Information):** 1,000 respondents in each country shown factual information about a COVID-19 vaccine.

### Debriefing
All respondents exposed to misinformation were debriefed after the survey. Debriefing information is provided in the questionnaire included in Supplementary Information.

### Recoding of Respondent Characteristics
Some respondent characteristics were recoded to reduce their number and facilitate comparison across the two countries. Details are provided in Supplementary Table 8 and Supplementary Table 9.

### Selection of Images
The images shown to respondents should meet several criteria:
1. **Relevance:** Recent and relevant to a COVID-19 vaccine.
2. **Engagement:** High engagement through user reach or other publicity, representing information likely exposed through social media use.
3. **Familiarity:** Posts shared by organizations or people familiar to the audience (e.g., US and UK audiences not shown unfamiliar individuals).
4. **Distinctiveness:** Unique content not replicating existing misinformation.

### Treatment Set
A COVID-19 vaccine-specific Boolean search query was used to extract online information from June 1, 2020, to August 30, 2020:
- Query: `corona* OR coronavirus OR covid* OR 'wuhan virus' OR wuhanvirus OR 'Chinese virus' OR 'china virus' OR chinavirus OR 'nCoV*' OR SARS-CoV*) AND vaccin* AND (Gates OR 5 G OR microchip OR 'New World Order' OR cabal OR globali*)`
- Source: Meltwater, an online social media listening platform.

### Control Set
The aim was to expose people to factual COVID-19 vaccine information as a control against misinformation exposure. Reputable online sources of knowledge were consulted to determine which content was classified as misinformation.

### Misinformation Examples
- **US Sample:** Post falsely claiming that a COVID-19 vaccine will alter DNA in humans.
- **UK Sample:** Post falsely claiming that a COVID-19 vaccine will cause 97% of recipients to become infertile.

This methodology ensures that the study can effectively assess the impact of misinformation on vaccination intent while controlling for other factors.
